Angkor Wat

Angkor Wat is the most famous temple in Cambodia and one of the greatest architectural wonders in the world. It is in Siem Reap Province and was built in the early 12th century by King Suryavarman II during the time of the Khmer Empire. Originally, Angkor Wat was constructed as a Hindu temple dedicated to the god Vishnu, but later it became a Buddhist temple and remains an important religious site today.

The temple is known for its magnificent design, tall towers, beautiful stone carvings, and large surrounding moat. Angkor Wat was built using sandstone blocks and shows the incredible skill of Khmer architects and artists. The walls of the temple contain detailed carvings that tell stories from Hindu mythology and ancient Khmer history.

Angkor Wat is not only a symbol of Cambodia's rich culture and history, but it also appears on the national flag of Cambodia. Millions of tourists from around the world visit Angkor Wat every year to admire its beauty and learn about the ancient Khmer civilization.

In 1992, UNESCO declared Angkor Wat a World Heritage Site because of its historical and cultural importance.

Today, Angkor Wat remains one of the most important landmarks in Southeast Asia.
